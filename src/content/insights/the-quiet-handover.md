Succession is usually framed as an event: a date, a signature, a change of title. The families who manage it well treat it as a period instead, often a long one, in which responsibility moves gradually and visibly from one generation to the next.

The next generation rarely needs to be tested so much as trusted with small, real decisions early, and given room to make a few mistakes while the stakes are still low.

### Time is the instrument

Rushing a handover to meet a tax deadline or a board's impatience tends to produce the opposite of stability. The cost of going slowly is almost always lower than the cost of going wrong.

> A handover done in haste is remembered as a rupture. Done slowly, it is barely remembered at all.

The goal is not a clean break but a seam that holds — a transition the family experiences as continuity rather than loss.
