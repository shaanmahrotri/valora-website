## Core principle: visibility over convenience

For a family office, sovereign AI infrastructure means hardware, data, tools and decision rules under your control. This is not about building a technology department. It is about ensuring that:

1. **Data custody is explicit** - you know where sensitive information lives and who can access it
2. **Dependencies are visible** - you own the audit trail, not a vendor
3. **Decision governance is legible** - the logic that allocates capital, manages risk, or handles confidential information is transparent to your advisors
4. **Costs and risks are encoded** - what looks like "free" LLM access has hidden costs in control, auditability and data leakage

---

## Inbound information layer

The family office's data originates from multiple systems of record. These do not move to the cloud for analysis. Instead, they are ingested under controlled conditions: email and calendar, portfolio data, documents, accounting. Each carries its own governance question, and most family offices have never made these decisions deliberately. Email is the clearest example - the question is not "do we use email" but "what lives in that email" and "who has decided what's admissible for AI processing?"

---

## Processing layer: your environment

This is where data becomes intelligence under your control. Every component lives in infrastructure you operate or have explicitly audited. The point is not to avoid cloud services, but to avoid opaque, black-box processing - across ingestion and parsing, a memory layer, a local reasoning engine, agents and workflows, and audit and compliance.

A local LLM is the governance moat: no data leaves your environment for inference, and no third-party provider sees your portfolio or your principal's risk tolerance. The tradeoff is explicit - less capability for more control. That is a deliberate choice, not a limitation.

---

## Frontier models: called, not owned

Claude, GPT, Gemini and other frontier models live at the edge. They are called on when necessary, in a governed manner, after local processing - for breadth of knowledge, for reasoning too complex for a local model, for one-off analysis where the cost is justified by the insight. Every call should be logged, scoped, and minimised to the excerpt actually needed, never the full document.

---

## What this architecture costs

**In capital:** vector database £5k-£20k annually, local LLM infrastructure £2k-£10k monthly, audit and logging £1k-£5k monthly, integration and engineering time £80k-£250k one-time.

**In control:** you own the decision log. You can explain every recommendation an AI made, audit which investments were influenced by it, and change your mind about how an agent should behave without waiting for a vendor update.

**In risk:** your infrastructure is visible, therefore your risks are visible. If an advisor asks "is my analysis being shared with Claude," you can answer honestly.

---

## What your board should ask

The question is not "should we use AI?" It is "on what data, in what way, under what controls?"

If the answer is "we are using ChatGPT and it is convenient," that is a decision: convenience over control. Sometimes the right one. It should be made deliberately, not by default.

*Sovereignty costs money and operational effort. It also buys you control - and control is the thing a family office cannot outsource.*
