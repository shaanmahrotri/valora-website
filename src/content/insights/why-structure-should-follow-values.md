There is a sequence most family offices get the wrong way round. They begin with the portfolio, because the portfolio is what exists, and they build governance to manage it. Identity, if it is discussed at all, arrives last, as a line in a mission statement.

Start instead with the family. Who they are, what they value, how they want to make decisions and live alongside one another. Only then does it make sense to ask what the structure should do.

### Identity is not soft

Advisers tend to treat values as a warm-up before the real work of policy. In practice they are the policy. A family that knows what it is for will write a clearer investment policy, choose better managers, and argue less about the things that do not matter.

> Structure, governance and investment policy all perform differently when they emerge from a clear sense of who the family is.

None of this is quick, and it is not meant to be. It is the foundation, and a foundation is the one part of the work you do not return to. Get it right, and everything built on it holds its shape.
