Most governance frameworks fail not because they are wrong but because they are too much. A calendar of quarterly committees, annual reviews and standing sub-groups looks rigorous on paper and collapses by the second year.

A calendar that families keep to is short. A small number of meetings that genuinely decide things, scheduled far enough ahead that they survive contact with a busy year.

### Fewer, better kept

Cadence matters more than completeness. A modest rhythm that actually happens builds more trust than an elaborate structure that lapses after the first difficult quarter.

> Governance that is never skipped teaches a family that decisions get made. Governance that lapses teaches the opposite.

Design the calendar for the year you will actually have, not the one in the framework. The discipline is in keeping it.
