Oversight is often confused with reporting. Reports arrive on schedule, full of figures that confirm the world is roughly as expected. Oversight is the harder discipline of knowing which figures matter and which questions they are quietly avoiding.

Independence is the part families most often underbuild. An overseer who depends on the manager for access, or for their next engagement, is not independent in the way that counts.

### Ask better questions

The value of good oversight is rarely in catching fraud, which is rare. It is in surfacing drift — the slow divergence between what a mandate says and what a portfolio actually does.

> The point of oversight is not to distrust your managers. It is to be able to trust them for the right reasons.

Done well, oversight makes the relationship with a manager better, not adversarial. It replaces vague comfort with specific confidence.
